# How to Create a Meaningful Life Goals List Instead of a Bucket List

Let's face it: bucket lists and goals are completely different. Goals are accomplished and celebrated; the end of a bucket list implies a final countdown. When you shift your mindset from a standard bucket list to an active **bucket list of life goals**, you transform passive wishes into intentional milestones. So let's kick the traditional bucket list and build an empowering goal bucket list instead. Here's how to get started.

## 1. What do You Care about?

![What do You Care about?](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/dx/th/k797psh256edb655e8ee1341743425_500x500.jpg)

I'm a runner, so naturally, my goals list includes running-related achievements like running a marathon and completing a race in every state. Who are you at your core? What hobbies and interests make you feel alive? Base your core **bucket list goals** around the activities that bring you genuine joy and fulfillment.

To help organize your thoughts, try grouping your initial ideas into distinct categories:

- **Personal Growth:** Learning new skills or languages
- **Fitness & Health:** Endurance challenges or wellness routines
- **Creative Pursuits:** Writing, crafting, or photography

## 2. Include Travel

![Include Travel](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/du/l0/ytxraeym56edb67ac77d6417806639_600x600.jpg)

Running a marathon is great, but adding travel turns a simple routine into a true adventure. That's why I decided to combine travel with running by setting a goal to run a race in every state. Your travel goals don't have to cover every state, though; you could aim to explore national parks managed by the [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov), visit every continent, or explore hidden gems across your home county. If you want to share these journeys, consider [planning a meaningful trip with family](#3) to create lasting shared experiences.

## 3. Include Family

![Include Family](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/gv/oq/u16ffb5x56eda0b62a6ca867228928_600x600.jpg)

It's also a fantastic idea to include your family in your life goals. For instance, you could add a goal of taking a road trip to the place where your grandmother was born—or even better, take that trip alongside her. Involving loved ones creates deeper connections and turns personal milestones into family legacies.

## 4. Include Friends

![Include Friends](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/ss/wm/glgwsf6d56eda0e0ab668514906840_600x600.jpg)

This idea of including your friends in your life goals is probably where #squadgoals originated, and for good reason! Creating a **best friend goals list** gives you shared experiences to look forward to together. You'll be so glad you took the time to build lifelong memories with the people who support you every day.

Here are a few popular shared activities to consider for your list:

- Planning an annual weekend cabin retreat
- Taking a cooking or pottery class together
- Training for a fun 5K obstacle course

## 5. Include Lovers

![clothing, leisure, swimming pool, swimwear, sun tanning,](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/ec/gh/oaok3ylh58a365bb3b9c7010044835_600x634.jpg)

I have a friend who set a unique goal of dating someone who spoke a different language while traveling abroad. Because she traveled frequently, it wasn't such an odd idea, and it pushed her out of her comfort zone. Whether it's learning a new dance style together or exploring a new country, ensure your list includes romantic adventures to experience with your partner.

## 6. DIY

![DIY](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/eo/js/v4jnmbxw56edb6ac86334029206157_500x500.jpg)

While shared experiences are wonderful, some of your goals ought to be just for you, alone. Personal endeavors build independence and self-reliance. For example, I tackle most of my long-distance races by myself, though in states where I have friends and family, I run shorter races with them. Having solo goals allows you to focus purely on your own personal growth.

## 7. Track Your Progress

![Track Your Progress](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/av/rv/xcnj294c56edb38eae302446975864_500x500.jpg)

Goals need to be written down to feel real, tangible, and actionable. Write them down in a dedicated notebook or use activity tracking tools like [Strava](https://www.strava.com) for fitness targets. Review your list regularly, celebrate incremental wins, and check off milestones as you attain them.

## 8. Be Flexible

![Be Flexible](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/zy/m0/m6y1fv4o56edb9ad2e303728022853_600x600.jpg)

So many fantastic books have been written about goals that didn't always happen as planned—Cheryl Strayed's memoir [Wild](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/13641789-wild) comes to mind. Be flexible in your **goals bucket list**, and achieve them even when circumstances change along the way.

## 9. Make It (somewhat) Realistic

![Make It (somewhat) Realistic](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/x5/ss/jlmivle656edb73cb0f68181878596_600x600.jpg)

If you love mountaineering, your heart might tell you to set the goal of climbing each of the tallest mountains on every continent. That's amazing... but probably not realistic, not just from a physical standpoint, but financially as well. Your goals shouldn't be easy, but don't make them impossible either. Remember to [stay flexible with your expectations](#8) as life evolves.

## 10. Keep Mementos

![Keep Mementos](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/e1/c9/w27dy1ai56edb5edf2abd830739600_500x500.jpg)

I keep all of my race bibs and finish-line photos to remember each milestone. My partner, whose goal is to visit a microbrewery in each state, keeps a pint glass from each one. Keep mementos of each stop along your journey—notes, photos, and physical keepsakes—so when you look back years from now, you'll have tangible items to match your memories.

## 11. It's Not a Checklist

![It's Not a Checklist](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/t0/lx/t85hire056edb9730dfd2892093318_600x600.jpg)

Here's the key thing about goals: they're not simple checklists (like bucket lists are). Goals evolve and should require continuous reflection and soul-searching. For instance, when I first started running, my goal was "run a 5k without stopping." Now it's "run a marathon without stopping." Don't just visit the Eiffel Tower to tick it off your list and move on. Savor each experience, build on each milestone, and [cherish the mementos you collect](#10) before you proceed to the next adventure.

Here is a quick comparison between a traditional bucket list and an active life goals list:

| Approach | Primary Focus | Long-Term Mindset |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traditional Bucket List | Passive items to check off | Static list before dying |
| Active Life Goals List | Actionable milestones & growth | Evolving journey of achievements |

Do you have any personal **bucket list goals** on your list right now? What are they, and how do you stay motivated? What else would you add to my list of tips?
